2.3. Video Diusion Distilation
As shown in Tab. 1, recent video diusion distilation methods typicaly folow a two-stage pipeline: (1) forward
training for initialization and (2) on-policy distilation for renement. The rst stage improves optimization
stability, while the second reduces rolout drift during few-step inference. For example, rCM [9] initializes
from sCM [8] and then aplies consistency backward simulation [24] with distribution matching [25]. In causal
video generation, Self-Forcing [10] adopts data-fre consistency ODE initialization [26] before performing a
similar on-policy renement stage. Our method folows the same high-level pipeline but replaces consistency
modeling with a ow map formulation, enabling stronger any-step performance.
A concurent work, TMD [27], also studies a ow map formulation for bidirectional video diusion distilation.
The main dierence lies in how ecient rolout is achieved.
TMD improves rolout eciency from an
architectural perspective by sharing the backbone and introducing an aditional ow head. In contrast, our
method improves simulation eciency from the ow-trajectory perspective by decomposing a ful trajectory
into shortcut transitions based on the composition property of ow maps. As a result, our aproach naturaly
suports arbitrary step budgets and generalizes to both bidirectional and causal architectures.
Causal Video Diusion. A growing line of work studies causal video diusion models [28
 31]. These
models often suer from exposure bias, which leads to eror acumulation during autoregresive generation.
Self-Forcing [10] mitigates this isue through on-policy distilation, but its consistency-based formulation is
mainly tailored to few-step setings for rolout eciency. Another line of work [32] focuses more on multi-step
models by explicitly modeling test-time erors during pretraining, but it is not directly designed for ecient
few-step sampling. By contrast, our method starts from a ow map formulation and uses ow map backward
simulation to suport both few-step and multi-step causal video generation within a unied framework.
2.4. On-Policy Distilation
On-policy distilation mitigates test-time erors by training the student on its own generated trajectories and
supervising them with a stronger teacher. This aproach has demonstrated benets in both large language
models [335] and diusion models [10,24,25].
For diusion models, on-policy distilation is typicaly implemented via Distribution Matching Distilation
(DMD) [24,25] or adversarial distilation [36,37]. Self-Forcing [10] combines self-rolouts with distribution
matching against a bidirectional teacher, while APT2 [37] evaluates one-step video rolouts using a discriminator.
Our method folows the same on-policy distilation paradigm but introduces ow map backward simulation,
specicaly designed for the ow map formulation, enabling any-step video generation.

computationaly expensive because it requires roling out the ful trajectory.

We present AnyFlow, the rst any-step video diusion distilation framework based on a two-time ow
map formulation. By learning transitions betwen arbitrary time pairs, AnyFlow suports a wide range of
sampling budgets within a single model. Built on large pretrained video diusion backbones, it combines
an improved forward ow map training recipe with ow map backward simulation for on-policy distilation,
thereby reducing discretization eror and exposure bias during sampling. Acros both bidirectional and causal

Limitations. The primary limitation of our method is its reliance on external datasets for ow map training.
